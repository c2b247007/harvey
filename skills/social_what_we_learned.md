# What we learned

A short, true account of something the user got wrong or figured out. It is written in the first person, in the user's voice, for peers and buyers who like to know who they are dealing with.

## What you need from the user

The story, from them. Ask: what happened, what did you expect, what did you change. If they have no story this month, draft nothing. A made-up lesson reads as made up.

## Shape

1. What happened, plainly. One or two sentences.
2. What they thought at the time.
3. What they do differently now.
4. Stop. No moral, no question to the audience.

## By platform

- **LinkedIn:** 100 to 180 words. No one-word lines, no "Here's the thing".
- **X:** the turn of the story in one post, under 280 characters.

## Do not

- Blame a customer, a supplier or a competitor, named or not
- Turn the last line into a pitch
- Write in a voice the user would not use out loud
